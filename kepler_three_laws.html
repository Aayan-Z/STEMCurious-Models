Law 1 — Elliptical orbits

A planet follows an ellipse, not a perfect circle. The Sun sits at one focus, off to the side of the ellipse’s geometric centre.

Law 2 — Equal areas

A line from the Sun to the planet sweeps out equal areas in equal time intervals. The planet moves faster near the Sun and slower farther away.

Law 3 — Harmonic law

For bodies orbiting the same star, the square of the period grows in proportion to the cube of the semi-major axis:

T² / a³ = constant

Why the speed changes

Gravity continuously pulls the planet toward the Sun. In an elliptical orbit, that pull converts gravitational potential energy into kinetic energy as the planet approaches the Sun, then converts it back as the planet recedes. Angular momentum is also conserved, producing equal swept areas in equal times.
